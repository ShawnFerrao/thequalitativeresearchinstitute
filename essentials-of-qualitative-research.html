Overview

Build the knowledge and confidence to navigate qualitative research

Qualitative research offers powerful ways to explore people's experiences, perspectives and the meanings they make of the world. But if you're new to qualitative research, the concepts, terminology and methodological choices can feel overwhelming.

Where do you begin? How does qualitative research differ from quantitative research? What counts as qualitative data? And how do you move from collecting data to developing meaningful interpretations?

In this comprehensive, self-paced course, Dr Claire Moran guides you through the essential foundations of qualitative research, combining clear explanations with practical examples and guided learning activities.

You'll explore the principles that underpin qualitative research, learn about different approaches to generating and analysing data, and develop an understanding of how research questions, theoretical perspectives and methodological decisions fit together.

Designed to be accessible, engaging and practical, this course will help you build the knowledge and confidence to take your next steps in qualitative research.

Who it's for

Who is this course for?

Essentials of Qualitative Research is designed for anyone seeking a clear, accessible introduction to qualitative research. It is particularly suited to:

  • PhD and postgraduate researchers beginning their qualitative research journey.
  • Undergraduate and honours students studying qualitative research for the first time.
  • Academic researchers transitioning from quantitative to qualitative research.
  • Research professionals and practitioners who want to develop foundational qualitative research skills.
  • Anyone seeking to refresh or strengthen their understanding of the principles of qualitative research.

No previous experience with qualitative research is required.

Content

What does the course cover?

Understanding the foundations of qualitative research

We'll begin by exploring what qualitative research is and what makes it distinctive.

You'll learn about the differences between qualitative and quantitative research, including the types of questions each approach is suited to answering.

We'll also explore some of the defining characteristics of qualitative research, including its attention to meaning, context, subjectivity and the complexity of human experience.

Exploring meaning and social context

Qualitative research is concerned with more than simply describing what people think or do.

We'll examine how meaning operates across different levels of human experience, including:

  • Individual experiences, emotions and perspectives.
  • Interpersonal relationships and immediate social contexts.
  • Broader cultural, institutional and societal influences.

Through practical examples, you'll develop an understanding of how these interconnected systems of meaning can inform qualitative research questions and interpretations.

Collecting and generating qualitative data

Qualitative research draws on a wide range of data sources, extending well beyond interviews and focus groups.

You'll explore the distinction between producing new data for a research project and selecting existing materials for analysis. We'll introduce different approaches to qualitative data collection, including:

  • Individual interviews and focus groups.
  • Observation and ethnographic approaches.
  • Participant diaries and creative methods.
  • Documents, media and archival materials.
  • Visual and other forms of qualitative data.

You'll also consider the importance of open-ended questioning, flexibility and the researcher's role in generating meaningful data.

Introduction to qualitative coding and theme development

Once you've collected qualitative data, how do you begin making sense of it? This section introduces the foundations of qualitative data analysis, focusing on the processes of coding and developing themes.

Using a sample dataset, you'll explore how researchers:

  • Engage actively with qualitative data.
  • Identify meaningful segments of data.
  • Develop initial codes.
  • Consider relationships and patterns across codes.
  • Begin organising codes into potential themes.
  • Develop meaningful and informative theme names.

Through guided exercises and worked examples, you'll gain an introductory understanding of how qualitative researchers move from raw data towards analytical interpretations.

This course provides a foundation in coding and theme development rather than comprehensive training in a particular analytical methodology.

If you're planning to use thematic analysis in your research, our Reflexive Thematic Analysis Masterclass provides more in-depth training in Braun and Clarke's approach, including the full analytical process, reflexivity and methodological quality.

Developing qualitative research questions

A strong research question is the starting point for a coherent research project.

We'll explore what makes a good qualitative research question and how qualitative questions differ from those typically used in quantitative research. You'll consider:

  • Why qualitative research questions are often exploratory and process-oriented.
  • The importance of remaining open to unexpected findings.
  • How research questions may evolve during a qualitative study.
  • The relationship between research questions and methodological choices.
  • The ethical and social implications of the knowledge produced through research.

The focus is on understanding how research questions guide the decisions researchers make throughout a study.

Understanding theoretical perspectives in qualitative research

Concepts such as ontology, epistemology and theoretical perspectives can be challenging when you're first encountering qualitative research. In this section, we'll break down these concepts and explore why they matter.

You'll be introduced to different ways of understanding reality and knowledge, including realist, critical realist and relativist perspectives, alongside essentialist, contextualist and constructionist approaches. We'll consider how theoretical assumptions influence:

  • What researchers understand as reality and knowledge.
  • The kinds of research questions they ask.
  • How they engage with participants and data.
  • The interpretations they develop.
  • The claims they can make from their research.

You'll also explore the importance of researcher subjectivity and reflexivity, including how researchers' experiences, assumptions and perspectives shape the research process.

If you're preparing to develop a qualitative study, our Qualitative Research Design Masterclass builds on these foundations, providing comprehensive guidance on research questions, methodology, methods and developing a coherent, defensible research design.
Approach

How will you learn?

This is not a course where you simply watch six hours of presentations.

The training combines clear explanations with practical exercises, reflection activities, quizzes and worked examples to help you consolidate your learning. Throughout the course, Claire draws on real-world examples to make complex methodological concepts accessible and relevant.

You'll have opportunities to pause the recordings, complete activities and compare your responses with the sample answers provided. The course is designed to build your understanding progressively, connecting theoretical concepts with practical applications.

And because it's self-paced, you can work through the material at a time and pace that suits you, revisiting sections whenever you need to.
